The record · ESG and transparency

Receipts, not ratings.

Every container we move writes four signed facts — where it came from, what it is made of, what it cost the planet, and who was paid — to a record that cannot be edited. ESG evidence is what the record produces, not what we write about it afterwards.

In plain words

ESG failed on evidence, not intent.

Ratings built from questionnaires. Claims nobody could check. Credits that cost less than the paper they were printed on.

Regulators have stopped accepting that. Three continents now ask for proof per shipment: a carbon price at the European border, forced-labour tracing at the American border, deforestation-free proof for goods entering Europe. A chain that cannot see itself is being ordered to testify.

Our founders spent twenty years moving hundreds of millions of dollars of product from factory to distribution centre , and learned the one thing no report says: the truth about a product is settled in the factory, long before anyone writes the label. So our answer is not a report. It is the record our trade already writes.

Regeneration

A supply chain that pays the planet back.

245trees individually verified through SunMint, October 2026
273.43 haunder restoration across 22 plots and 15 partner farms

Through SunMint, a programme run by our partner TrueSight, cacao farmers in the Brazilian Amazon plant native trees under forest canopy on already-degraded land — never standing forest.

Each tree is photographed at planting, located by GPS, signed by the person who planted it and recorded on chain. The farmer keeps the land and owns the trees; SunMint finances the eighteen months before cacao bears fruit. Every six months the planter submits two photographs per tree, so growth is measured for as long as the tree stands.

The programme already moves money to people: on 15 October 2026 it paid 86 students at a partner rural school R$5 per verified tree, directly, by PIX. The rule on every product is four words long: this unit funded this tree.

Said plainly: no registry-issued carbon credit exists yet. The contribution lane runs today on tree-level traceability. The credit lane — methodology, independent validation, registration — has not begun. A tree only ever sits in one lane. The programme's target is 10,000 hectares of Amazon restoration through cacao agroforestry.

The operating commitment

The gate we set ourselves.

By 2055, every supply chain Bionpact operates nets its audited emissions to zero against verified removal — measured from the record, published annually, replayable by anyone with the right to read it. Not a pledge. A number.

Our trade runs across one seam of the world: from the factory floor at one end to the retail shelf at the other, and both ends have a carbon clock. The economy where our goods are sold targets net zero by 2050; the economy where they are made commits to it by 2060. Neither deadline is bankable for a company that lives between them. A deadline you set yourself, on a record you cannot edit, is the only kind that binds — so we placed ours in the middle and tied it to the ledger that pays every contributor.

Planetary metrics enter the platform at three stages: materials selected from a certified registry at quotation; factory reduction programmes logged as production events; freight consolidated and audited leg by leg. Whatever remains after reduction is netted against verified removal — trees photographed, located and signed at planting, and independently validated before any credit is counted. What does not count: unverified credits however cheap, funded-tree counters presented as verified trees, estimates in place of events, and any instrument that could make the record lie.

BION 2055 is the near gate of a longer horizon the founders call BION 3000: the thousand-year question asked of a ledger — if the record were kept faithfully that long, what kind of person would grow up inside it? That is vision, marked as such, and it lives in the BION 3000 paper rather than on this site. What lives here is the receipt.
